# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is peers and curious thinkers who arrive through
writing, references, search, or direct sharing. They are interested in clear
thought across health, philosophy, systems thinking, engineering,
self-sovereignty, running, and practical learning. Some visitors may be
technical peers looking for finite element analysis resources or engineering
perspective; others may be general readers looking for a thoughtful personal
publication.

Visitors should quickly understand that this is Nick J Stevens' independent
home on the web: a place for essays, practical notes, technical resources, and
evolving personal context.

## Product Purpose

The site exists as a personal brand and self-sovereign publishing home. It
should make Nick's thinking easy to explore, present his writing and technical
work with care, and create a polished first impression without turning into a
corporate portfolio.

Success means a visitor can read, browse, and form a clear sense of the person
behind the work: reflective, practical, technically capable,
health-conscious, and intellectually curious.

## Brand Personality

Clean, polished, beautiful.

The tone should feel thoughtful, independent, precise, and humane. It should
carry the confidence of someone with engineering depth and lived experience,
while staying welcoming to readers outside a narrow technical niche.

## Anti-references

The site should not feel boring, cluttered, or corporate.

Avoid generic SaaS polish, dense academic CV presentation,
influencer-newsletter tropes, and consultancy brochure language. The interface
should not flatten the personal voice into stock professionalism or make the
breadth of topics feel chaotic.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with independent voice. The site should feel like a person with
   judgment and taste, not a template wrapped around content.
2. Make breadth feel intentional. Health, philosophy, systems thinking,
   engineering, running, and family writing should feel connected by curiosity
   and practice rather than scattered.
3. Preserve reading quality. Long-form content is central, so typography,
   spacing, navigation, and page rhythm must make reading calm and effortless.
4. Show practical depth. Engineering resources and tools should feel credible
   and useful without overpowering the personal publication.
5. Polish without corporate gloss. The site should be refined and beautiful,
   but never sterile, salesy, or overproduced.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific formal accessibility requirement has been set. Future interface
work should still protect readable typography, strong contrast, clear focus
states, and sensible document structure. Keyboard navigation is a valued
nice-to-have, especially for navigation, search, menus, and interactive tools.
